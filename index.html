Our Story

Still doing it
the way Patrick
did.

Patrick Hartnett opened the shop in 1962 with a single block, a set of knives, and a handshake deal with a farmer two miles up the road. Sixty-two years later the block is older, the knives are sharper, and most of those handshake deals are still in place — just with the farmers' grandchildren.

We work directly with five local farms, hang our beef for a minimum of 28 days, and cut everything to order. Nothing pre-packed. Nothing flown in. If we don't know where it came from, we don't sell it.

The Craft

Farm to
block, in
four steps.

No middlemen, no central packing plant. Four steps between the field and your kitchen — and we can name every person involved in each one.

  1. 01

    Five farms, thirty miles

    Ballymore, Ardra, Coolbawn, Glenview and O'Brien's of Cloyne. Denis drives out to each of them himself. Every animal we sell was raised inside a thirty-mile ring around the shop.

  2. 02

    Twenty-eight days hanging

    Beef goes on the hook in our own chill and stays there a minimum of four weeks. It loses weight and gains flavour — which is exactly the trade the supermarkets won't make.

  3. 03

    Broken down by hand

    Whole carcass, one block, three sets of hands. Seaming out a forequarter properly takes practice you can't buy — Denis learned it from his father, and Ciara is learning it now.

  4. 04

    Cut to your order

    Nothing sits pre-wrapped in a tray. Tell us the thickness, the trim, how many it's feeding and when you're eating it — we'll cut it that morning and have it wrapped for collection.
