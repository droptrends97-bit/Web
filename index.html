02 — Our Story

Still doing it the way Patrick did.

Patrick's original block is still in the shop — third one back, worn a full inch lower in the middle. Denis won't replace it.

Patrick Hartnett opened the shop in 1962 with a single block, a set of knives, and a handshake deal with a farmer two miles up the road. Sixty-two years later the block is older, the knives are sharper, and most of those handshake deals are still in place — just with the farmers' grandchildren.

His son Denis took over in 1988, the week after Patrick's hands gave out. He kept the suppliers, kept the prices written on brown paper, and added a chill out the back so the beef could hang properly instead of going out on the Friday it came in.

Ciara joined in 2019, third generation, and she is the one who finally got the orders written down somewhere other than the back of a receipt roll. She is also the reason the sausage recipe now has mace in it.

We work directly with five local farms, hang our beef for a minimum of 28 days, and cut everything to order. Nothing pre-packed. Nothing flown in. If we don't know where it came from, we don't sell it.

The short version

Four things
we won't do.

Most of what makes a butcher good is a list of shortcuts they've refused to take.

  • 01

    No pre-packed trays

    Nothing sits under film waiting for you. It's cut when you ask for it, to the thickness you ask for.

  • 02

    No imported beef

    Five farms, all within thirty miles. We can tell you the herd and usually the field.

  • 03

    No water in the bacon

    Dry-cured on the premises. It browns in the pan instead of boiling itself grey.

  • 04

    No labels we can't explain

    If a sticker claims something, one of us can tell you exactly what it means. Otherwise it's off.

04 — Farm to block

Four steps between the field and your kitchen.

No middlemen, no central packing plant. We can name every person involved in each one.

  1. 01

    Five farms, thirty miles

    Ballymore, Ardra, Coolbawn, Glenview and O'Brien's of Cloyne. Denis drives out to each of them himself. Every animal we sell was raised inside a thirty-mile ring around the shop.

    Roughly 40 min in the van, worst case

  2. 02

    Twenty-eight days hanging

    Beef goes on the hook in our own chill and stays there a minimum of four weeks. It loses weight and gains flavour — which is exactly the trade the supermarkets won't make.

    Loses about 12% of its weight

  3. 03

    Broken down by hand

    Whole carcass, one block, three sets of hands. Seaming out a forequarter properly takes practice you can't buy — Denis learned it from his father, and Ciara is learning it now.

    Two and a half hours a side

  4. 04

    Cut to your order

    Nothing sits pre-wrapped in a tray. Tell us the thickness, the trim, how many it's feeding and when you're eating it — we'll cut it that morning and have it wrapped for collection.

    Order by 4pm the day before
